Pepsi x Peeps: how a giveaway became a grocery-store soda.

A yellow bottle tells only part of the story. Before marshmallow Pepsi reached store shelves, it was a prize most people could not simply buy.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished November 18, 20267 minute read
The short answer

Same collaboration. Two very different ways to get it.

Pepsi x Peeps debuted as a promotional giveaway in 2021, then received a limited nationwide retail release in 2023. The 20 oz bottle in our store belongs to the seller-described 2023 stock, not the original giveaway. This is a historical guide, not a new-launch announcement.

2021: an announcement was not a shopping invitation

Pepsi announced the collaboration on March 25, 2021. It paired cola with a marshmallow flavor and presented 7.5 oz mini cans in yellow, pink and blue. Access ran through the #HangingWithMyPEEPS sweepstakes, asking fans to share springtime photos rather than find the drink on a supermarket shelf.

That distinction changes how we should read an old headline. A product can be real, photographed professionally and discussed widely without being something a customer can order. Someone who remembers looking for it and never seeing it may be remembering the distribution model accurately, not overlooking an obvious store display.

For a collector, the original question was therefore not just which flavor to choose. It was whether a piece of promotional packaging could be obtained at all. That makes provenance important: a later bottle with the same collaboration name is not automatically an example of the first release. A good collection record should identify the format and release chapter separately.

2023: the comeback changed access

On February 14, 2023, Pepsi announced the first retail release: limited-time nationwide distribution in 20 oz bottles and 7.5 oz mini-can multipacks. The company described the earlier promotion as having reached 3,000 fans. That number describes the earlier audience, not a stated total production count for every can or later bottle.

This is why the comeback is more interesting than a simple reprint. The practical hurdle changed from winning access to finding a participating retailer. A customer could make the decision at a shelf, alongside ordinary drinks, instead of interpreting a social-media promotion. The collaboration became something that could be encountered casually.

Our interpretation is that the second chapter tests a different kind of interest. Wanting to win a scarce novelty and choosing to buy it during a normal shopping trip are not identical behaviors. Neither the existence of the giveaway nor the later rollout tells us how often customers bought it again. We do not have sales or repeat-purchase data that would answer that question.

The promise was marshmallow cola, not liquid candy

The useful distinction is between a flavor description and a claim about ingredients or texture. Marshmallow cola names the intended flavor concept. It does not, by itself, tell you whether the product contains a particular candy ingredient, meets a dietary preference, or recreates the texture of a marshmallow. Those are separate questions for the actual label.

As an editorial reading, the appeal of the idea is the contrast. Cola arrives with one set of expectations; a familiar seasonal candy brings another. The combination gives someone a reason to ask how two recognizable things might work together. That curiosity can exist even for a person who is not already a dedicated soda collector.

We are deliberately not filling that gap with invented tasting notes. We have not opened this bottle for a controlled comparison. Words such as smooth, toasted or vanilla-heavy would imply an observation we have not made. A historical guide can explain what the makers proposed without pretending to report what a particular older bottle tastes like now.

The cola sat inside a larger Peeps season

The Peeps brand's March 16, 2023 partnership announcement placed the returning cola alongside footwear, nail polish, plush products, cereal and a protein-powder collaboration. The beverage was one part of a wider seasonal program, not an isolated experiment involving only soda.

That wider context helps explain why the packaging is worth looking at even after a promotion ends. A can or bottle can act as a record of a collaboration across categories. You do not need to collect every associated object for the connection to matter; one item can document how a familiar brand appeared somewhere unexpected.

The distinction also keeps our claims modest. A broad partnership program is evidence of a coordinated brand presence, not proof that each individual product was a commercial success. We can discuss the creative strategy without inventing revenue, customer satisfaction or a reason for every product leaving a shelf.

How to identify the chapter you are collecting

Start with the container, then the wording, then the date evidence. A listing title that says rare Pepsi is not enough. Record whether you are looking at a mini can or a 20 oz bottle, whether the sale includes a carton, and whether the photos show the actual item or only an example of the design. Those details make two superficially similar listings meaningfully different.

A production or best-by marking deserves its own photo. The year in a seller title, the date a listing was created and the date printed on a bottle serve different purposes. A newly relisted bottle does not become a new production run. When a marking is unclear, preserve what it actually says instead of turning an uncertain code into a confident calendar date.

For a display, ask about the cap, seal, label alignment, scuffs and bottle shape. These questions concern the object you will receive, not merely whether a flavor once existed. A front-facing image is a useful beginning, but it does not answer every condition question a collector may care about.

An old promotion is not a current availability promise

Release history, current production and resale availability are different records. The historical announcements establish how the product reached people at particular times. A current resale listing establishes that a seller is offering an item. Neither automatically proves a new factory run, an annual return or a permanent end to the flavor in every market.

This guide therefore does not announce a 2026 comeback or attach an exact final production date. We have not established either. If you encounter an old article written in the present tense, check its publication date before treating its store-finder directions or promotional offer as current.

The same care applies to perceived rarity. A high asking price is a seller's offer, not a completed sale or a reliable valuation. Choose a collectible because its format, design and documented history fit your interests. We are not presenting an older bottle as an investment or promising that its value will increase.

The bottle available through Discontinued Club

Our Pepsi x Peeps product page describes a single full 20 oz bottle from 2023. It is older stock, not a fresh delivery from the manufacturer. Current availability and the purchase total belong on that page and at checkout, so we are not fixing a stock count inside this article.

Before buying, request the printed date and current condition photos. Full or unopened describes the item but does not establish freshness, carbonation or suitability for drinking. Someone who wants the original tasting experience needs to resolve those questions rather than assume that a sealed collectible provides it.

What the bottle can reliably invite is a closer look at the history: a collaboration that people first had to win, then could find in stores. Separating those chapters makes the object more interesting, not less. It lets the real story carry the appeal without a claim that this is the last bottle anywhere.
