Starbucks' Aerocano has arrived. It isn't Nitro Cold Brew.

The cascading, foamy coffee is joining the North American menu today. Here is what separates the espresso drink from cold brew, and why international menu photos can be misleading.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished September 29, 20264 minute read
The short answer

Aerocano is a confirmed U.S. launch, not another rumor.

Starbucks says Aerocano launches in North America on September 29, 2026, in original and Caramel versions as part of its core menu. The drink uses air-whipped Blonde Espresso and ice, producing a foamy top and a cascading appearance.

What is an Aerocano?

The North American explainer presents it as an air-whipped take on an iced Americano. Its foam and visible cascade come from the preparation, rather than simply describing a cold coffee topped with a separately ordered flavored foam.

For someone choosing from a menu photo, that distinction is useful. A creamy-looking surface is not enough to identify a recipe. Read the drink name and its ingredients; do not use the color of the foam to decide whether a particular order contains milk, a syrup or another addition.

Aerocano versus Nitro Cold Brew: the coffee and process differ

Starbucks' Australian Aerocano explainer distinguishes air-whipping from nitrogen infusion. Similar-looking bubbles do not make the two drinks equivalent.

The U.S. Nitro Cold Brew menu page describes a slow-steeped cold brew infused with nitrogen. Aerocano starts with espresso instead. Those are different coffee preparations, even if a quick social-media clip makes both look like a dark drink with a pale head.

It follows that a Nitro review is not automatically a review of Aerocano. We would also avoid transferring one drink's caffeine or calorie figures to the other. Recipe, serving size and any added flavoring matter more than a visual resemblance.

Which versions are confirmed for the United States?

The September 28 North American announcement names Aerocano and Caramel Aerocano. Those are the versions supported by the U.S.-relevant release we reviewed. We have not verified additional American variants from foreign menu screenshots.

For example, the Australian page also discusses Yuzu and Honey Ruby Grapefruit versions. That is evidence about Australia, not a U.S. launch announcement. International reporting can be accurate and still answer a different shopping question from "can I order this at my local American store?"

Why have people already seen it overseas?

Starbucks' February 24 Korea announcement scheduled an Aerocano debut there for February 26, 2026. It described a permanent-menu addition in that market.

That earlier release explains why the name and photos can feel familiar before the American launch. An older overseas post is not proof that a U.S. location was selling it months ago. When a product moves between markets, dates need their country attached.

This is also why we do not call September 29 the invention of Aerocano or its first appearance anywhere. The relevant news for our readers is the North American menu expansion, not a claim that the underlying idea has no history.

Is Aerocano just a fall special?

Starbucks calls the North American drink a core-menu addition. Its arrival during a fall promotion should not be used as evidence that it disappears after Halloween.

The company's August 31 fall preview also scheduled Mini Apple Crumble, Mini Pecan and Mini Pumpkin Pies for September 29. Sharing a release date does not establish that the drinks and pastries have identical menu lifespans.

We have not verified a single nationwide price, a U.S. nutrition breakdown for every size or availability at each licensed location. Those are details to check against the current local menu, not blanks to fill using a different country's ordering page.

How to read this status

Confirmed launch means this is supported by the company's own announcement, not an anonymous menu leak. It does not mean that we have checked ordering availability at every location.

Menu status is also distinct from a guarantee of indefinite supply. A core-menu addition is different from an explicitly dated seasonal run, but future changes would require a new status check.

What to check when ordering

Select your actual store before relying on an app menu, price or pickup estimate. Confirm whether you want the original drink or the flavored version, and review the ingredients of any customization rather than assuming that all drinks sharing a name have identical labels.

This is coffee-news coverage, not a Discontinued Club product listing. We do not sell the drink, have not reviewed its taste and are not promising that your nearest store can make it today. A local unavailable message would not, by itself, contradict a national launch.

Is Aerocano the same as Nitro Cold Brew?

No. Aerocano is an air-whipped espresso drink; Nitro Cold Brew uses cold-brewed coffee infused with nitrogen.

Are the Australian flavors confirmed for the U.S.?

Not by the North American announcement reviewed here. Foreign menus should not be treated as U.S. availability lists.
