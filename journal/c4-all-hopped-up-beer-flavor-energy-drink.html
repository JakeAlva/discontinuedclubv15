C4 made a beer-inspired energy drink. Without the alcohol.

All Hopped Up is an actual launch, not a joke can. The surprising part is the flavor; the important shopping detail is that the announcement and current store page do not show the same price.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished September 28, 20264 minute read
The short answer

Yes, C4 All Hopped Up is real. It is not beer.

Nutrabolt announced All Hopped Up on September 23, 2026 as an alcohol-free energy drink inspired by light beer and orange. Its official Cellucor product page is live. The launch is confirmed, but that does not guarantee stock at every seller.

What exactly is All Hopped Up?

The manufacturer's announcement describes a light-beer-inspired taste with an orange accent. That is a flavor concept, not a claim that the can contains beer. The product sits in C4's energy-drink range.

It is an unusual combination because beer imagery creates one expectation and an energy-drink label creates another. Our reading is simple: use the product category and label to identify it, not the visual joke. We cannot tell you how close it tastes to a lager without trying it, and we have not done that.

Alcohol-free does not mean caffeine-free

C4's official product page lists 200 mg of caffeine and zero sugar. Its warnings identify the drink as intended for adults, not children, and say not to consume it during pregnancy or nursing.

That distinction matters more than the novelty. This is not a caffeine-free soda or simply a replacement label for ordinary nonalcoholic beer. Follow the package directions and consider the full label rather than treating an alcohol-free claim as a general suitability claim.

When did it launch, and where should you look?

The September 23 announcement named Amazon and TikTok Shop as the initial online channels, with C4Energy.com scheduled for September 30. Those are announced rollout dates, not a promise that orders placed today will ship immediately.

As of our September 28 check, the official Cellucor listing was already visible but marked unavailable. Because the stated direct-site date is still ahead, that status is not evidence that the new flavor has been discontinued. We have not verified shelf availability at individual stores or completed a checkout at either marketplace.

Why we are not quoting one guaranteed six-pack price

Nutrabolt's release quoted $14.99 for six 16-ounce cans. The official product listing we checked instead displayed $27.99 for a six-pack. We could not resolve the difference, so neither figure should be presented as a guaranteed price across all sellers.

At those two pack prices, the before-shipping cost works out to about $2.50 or $4.67 per can. That is a meaningful difference for the same stated quantity. Check the final offer rather than assuming a higher-priced result is a larger pack or that a launch promotion still applies.

Is it limited edition or here to stay?

We have not verified a permanent-lineup commitment or a final production date in the material reviewed. A fall-themed launch is not, by itself, enough to declare that the product disappears after Halloween. Nor does an unavailable listing tell us whether another batch is coming.

For readers following unusual flavors, the useful next evidence would be a specific distribution update, a restock or a manufacturer statement about the run. Until then, the supported story is the launch itself. Future scarcity and resale value are not established facts.

How to read this status

Confirmed release means the manufacturer has announced the product and published a matching listing. It is a different evidence level from a social-media concept, a fan render or an unnamed distributor rumor.

Availability remains a separate question. A real release can be unavailable from one seller while another channel has stock. Equally, an order button or a product page is not proof that every local store carries it.

Before ordering a pack

Compare the number of cans, can size, seller and delivered total. A search result can show a launch price while the linked listing has changed. Keep a screenshot of the offer you actually accept, especially if a marketplace combines several pack sizes on one page.

Discontinued Club is covering this as news and does not currently offer All Hopped Up for sale. The links here are reporting sources, not a claim that we have secured a shipment. We have not conducted a taste test and are not ranking it against beer or other energy drinks.
