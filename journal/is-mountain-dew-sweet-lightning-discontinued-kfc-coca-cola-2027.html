KFC is switching to Coke. What happens to Sweet Lightning?

The beverage partnership is official. A final day for the peach-and-honey Mountain Dew is not.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished October 16, 20264 minute read
The short answer

KFC has confirmed a supplier change, not a Sweet Lightning discontinuation date.

Coca-Cola announced the U.S. transition on October 15. The statement does not name Sweet Lightning or establish its final sale date, a new restaurant partner, or a retail release. Its future is a separate question.

When is KFC switching to Coca-Cola?

Coca-Cola's October 15 statement says the transition begins in January 2027, with its drinks expected across KFC's 3,600 U.S. restaurants by spring. That is a rollout window, not a claim that every restaurant changes fountains on New Year's Day.

For customers, the useful distinction is between a company announcement and a local menu. A future supply agreement does not tell you what a particular restaurant can pour this afternoon. We have not checked local machines, syrup stocks or conversion schedules.

Why Sweet Lightning is the flavor to watch

PepsiCo introduced Sweet Lightning on June 11, 2019, describing peach and honey flavors and nationwide KFC availability by July 1. The release positioned it as the chain's first exclusive beverage and a permanent fountain offering developed for its food.

That original positioning explains why this is more interesting than a routine cola swap. A drink associated with one restaurant has a different distribution question from a familiar supermarket soda: if that restaurant changes suppliers, where would customers find the exclusive next? That is the question we are following, not an answer we can invent.

Permanent also describes an intention at a point in time. It is not a promise that a restaurant, recipe or partnership can never change. The historical announcement is useful for understanding how the flavor arrived; it is not a guarantee of its future.

Is Mountain Dew Sweet Lightning being discontinued?

We cannot responsibly mark the flavor as already discontinued on October 16. Our reading is that a change away from its original beverage supplier puts the exclusive in question. That is an editorial inference from two official announcements, not confirmation that production has ended.

Several outcomes remain conceptually possible: the flavor could end, move to another outlet, or receive a different distribution format. None of those possibilities is a plan we have verified. In particular, a fan-made can image would not establish a supermarket launch, and a restaurant running out would not identify the last production date.

The missing evidence is specific: a statement naming Sweet Lightning, a dated product notice, or a clearly documented successor arrangement. Until then, a precise final day would create certainty the available sources do not provide.

Can you still order it at KFC?

Check the menu for the restaurant you actually intend to visit, then ask whether Sweet Lightning is available before making a special trip. We are not presenting a national stock check. A working menu button and an operating fountain are not the same piece of evidence.

If you send us a sighting, include the city, date and what changed: a temporary out-of-order sign, a missing menu entry or a replaced dispenser label. Those observations describe different situations. They are useful leads when identified accurately, but no single location can speak for the whole country.

Does this settle other Mountain Dew flavors?

No. A restaurant-specific supply story should not become a headline about the entire brand disappearing. Nor does this U.S. announcement establish what is happening in other countries. Different flavors and markets need their own evidence.

For another example of why geography matters, our Pitch Black U.K. launch report separates an overseas release from a U.S. comeback. Applying that same discipline here means tracking Sweet Lightning itself instead of treating the cola partnership as a complete flavor obituary.

How to read this status

The watch label belongs to Sweet Lightning's unresolved future, not to the existence of the Coca-Cola partnership. We will distinguish an announced exit from an already completed one if stronger evidence becomes available.
