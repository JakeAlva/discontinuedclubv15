New Wonka candy is coming to the U.S. in November.

Purple caramel, filled chocolate hats and raspberry-cola-centered gummies are part of a confirmed launch. The familiar name is back in the news, but this is a new collection, not proof that an old favorite has returned unchanged.

By Discontinued Club ResearchChecked September 28, 20264 minute read
The short answer

Four new Wonka treats are announced for November.

Ferrero North America's September 22 announcement schedules a limited-edition U.S. Wonka range for November 2026. It names Magic Hats chocolates, a Purplypurple chocolate bar, Magic Garden ropes and Oompa Loompa gummies. This is announced product news, not a rumor or a report that every store already has stock.

When is the new Wonka candy coming out?

The September 22 U.S. announcement gives November 2026 as the shelf window. Wonka's U.S. brand page also directs readers to look for the collection at retailers starting that month.

Neither of those statements is a guarantee that every store receives every product on one day. We have not verified a nationwide shelf date, complete retailer list or U.S. price list. As of September 28, this is a forward-looking shopping guide rather than a local stock check.

Purplypurple and Magic Hats: the chocolate side

The official U.S. product range describes Purplypurple as milk chocolate with a purple, caramel-flavored filling. Purple is the visual surprise here; it should not be read as a grape or berry flavor claim.

Magic Hats are filled, hat-shaped chocolates. The site shows 12-count and 18-count assortments with different flavor selections, so the larger box should not automatically be treated as simply more of an identical mix. Compare the assortment on the particular box if a specific filling is what interests you.

The candy side brings citrus and a cola twist

Ferrero describes Magic Garden Chewy Ropes as lime-flavored candy with a lemon center. Oompa Loompa Gummies combine blueberry and strawberry flavors with a raspberry-cola liquid filling. Those are the announced flavor descriptions, not observations from a taste test.

For readers arriving from our soda coverage, the cola-filled gummy is the most obvious crossover: a familiar drinks flavor in a different format. That is an editorial reason to follow it, not a claim that it recreates a particular soda. Texture and sweetness are questions we would need actual samples to evaluate.

Is this the old Wonka Bar coming back?

The evidence supports a new collection, not a verified reissue of any specific discontinued recipe. Ferrero's May 19 global announcement describes newly developed products and notes that the company acquired the confectionery rights in 2018.

That distinction is important on a site about missing favorites. A familiar brand name can return with different recipes, pack sizes and product families. To call something the same old bar, we would want an explicit recipe or product-identity statement, not just a purple wrapper and a nostalgic name.

Why international posts can make the timing confusing

Ferrero's global plan covered ten seasonal and limited-edition creations across several categories, with the U.S. and European markets included. That larger program is not identical to the four product families named in the later U.S. announcement.

An overseas shelf photo can therefore be useful evidence about that market without proving American availability. When checking a new Wonka post, look for the country, product name and date together. We are using the specific U.S. notice for this article's release window.

A Golden Ticket campaign is part of the rollout

The U.S. announcement also introduces a digital Golden Ticket promotion, connected to Ferrero's Netflix partnership. The official Wonka site currently asks visitors to return November 1 to enter. Use the brand's page for current rules and eligibility.

Do not confuse that campaign date with a confirmed shelf date for every candy. Participation, prizes and product availability are separate matters. We are linking to the organizer, not collecting entries, and a purchase should not be interpreted as a guaranteed ticket or prize.

How to read this status

Announced / November U.S. launch separates a supported future release from current store availability. A product can have final imagery and an official description before it reaches shoppers.

Limited edition also is not a precise end date. It signals a limited offer, but does not tell us when every retailer will sell its last pack or which individual item might eventually return.

What to check when listings appear

Start with the exact product name and the country of the listing. An overseas pack can be genuine without sharing the U.S. rollout date, serving information or retail offer. A preorder is not the same thing as an in-stock item ready to dispatch.

Discontinued Club is not taking orders for this range. We are reporting the announcement independently of our inventory. Before buying a food product, use the ingredient and allergen panel on the actual package rather than assuming that every item under the same brand has the same ingredients.
