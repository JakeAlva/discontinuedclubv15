Is Alani Nu Lime Slush discontinued?

The original limited release has run its course, but that does not establish permanent cancellation. New comeback claims are circulating, with no verified return date in the sources reviewed.

The short answer

The original limited run ended. A return is now rumored.

Lime Slush launched as a limited U.S. flavor in early 2026. Its original release window has passed, but we have not verified an exact last-production date or a permanent-cancellation notice. As of October 12, public posts claim a future return; we have not independently confirmed their dates or permanent-lineup claims.

October update: a comeback is being discussed

October 12, 2026: Public community posts mention a November 2026 return and a permanent 2027 comeback. We have not authenticated either as an official Alani Nu schedule. Read our Lime Slush return-rumor report for the dated claims and source checks.

We have revised this guide to avoid equating the end of a limited launch with permanent cancellation. Earlier wording was too definite about ongoing replenishment; the public sources do not establish the exact final production or distribution date.

What the limited-release label establishes

February launch coverage described Lime Slush as a limited edition. That supports treating the original spring launch as a limited run rather than assuming it was promised permanent shelf space.

Limited does not mean never again. A later seasonal launch, renewed distribution or permanent addition would be a separate development that needs its own evidence.

What the 2026 timeline shows

PepsiCo’s official SmartLabel database carried a Lime Slush product record updated in February 2026, and major-retailer reviews continued into the summer. Those sources establish that the flavor was genuinely circulating in the United States during 2026 rather than being an old rumor or mockup.

Later in the year, fans organized requests for Alani Nu to bring Lime Slush back. A campaign establishes consumer interest, not an exact production cutoff. Likewise, a missing product page alone cannot establish a permanent cancellation.

Retail and specialty inventory can outlast a limited run. A listing alone does not tell us its production date or prove a manufacturer relaunch. Stores and marketplace sellers can hold inventory for different lengths of time.

Check the seller’s photos, pack quantity, can condition, and date markings. Lime Slush was sold in several retail configurations, so a single can, a four-pack, and a case should not be treated as interchangeable listings.

Could Lime Slush come back?

Yes, but possibility is not confirmation. A dated Alani Nu announcement specifying the U.S. market and a release window would provide stronger evidence than a reposted schedule claim.

The new rumor report tracks the comeback claims separately from this historical guide. Our October 15 store drop is separate from any manufacturer relaunch.

What buyers and collectors should check

Verify the exact flavor name, package design, can size, country labeling, condition, quantity, and the seller's photo before buying. A marketplace listing can combine an old image with newer inventory, and a foreign-market can may use similar colors for a different formula. The can shown here is one specific reference design, not a promise that every listing uses the same package.

For older full cans, treat the purchase as a collectible first. Storage history is rarely complete, and sealed cans can leak, swell, or change internally over time. A printed date and intact seal help identify an item, but they do not guarantee that an aged beverage remains suitable to drink.
