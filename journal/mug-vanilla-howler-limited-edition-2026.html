MUG Vanilla Howler: the limited-edition float to look for.

A root beer float-inspired soda in a waffle-patterned can. The important shopping detail is whether the label says Original or Zero Sugar.

By Discontinued Club ResearchChecked October 5, 20264 minute read
The short answer

A real limited release, with two versions to distinguish.

MUG Root Beer Floats Vanilla Howler is a documented 2026 U.S. limited edition with Original and Zero Sugar options. Retail and distributor pages remain available at our October 5 check. Those pages identify the drink; they do not prove fresh stock at every store or a permanent extension.

What is MUG Vanilla Howler?

PepsiCo's June 8 announcement described a root beer and creamy vanilla flavor inspired by a float and the dirty-soda trend. It set June 14, 2026 as the U.S. retail arrival date and called this MUG's first limited-edition flavor release.

This is a packaged soda, not an order for a glass of root beer with a scoop of ice cream. Float describes the flavor idea. We have not tasted it and are not claiming that its texture or sweetness matches a freshly assembled float.

The full product name is useful here: MUG Root Beer Floats Vanilla Howler. Treat Floats and Vanilla Howler as identifying details, not optional words, when comparing a photo with a search result.

Original versus Zero Sugar: what the labels say

The Original 12-ounce can listing gives 160 calories and 43 grams of total sugars. Its ingredients include high fructose corn syrup, and the product is described as caffeine-free. This is the version pictured at the top of this article.

The Zero Sugar can listing gives zero calories and zero grams of sugars for the same serving size. It also identifies the soda as caffeine-free. These are two variants, not two conflicting labels for the same can.

Check the actual package if ingredients or serving size affect your choice. Similar flavor names and packaging do not make the formulas identical, and a retailer can group multiple variants under one product selector. Reading the selected option matters as much as recognizing the brand.

Where to look for Vanilla Howler in the U.S.

The launch announcement specified 12-ounce can multipacks and 20-ounce bottles for both versions. That gives shoppers more than one format to search for; a missing multipack does not automatically establish that no bottles remain in the area.

PepsiCo Partners lists a 24-can wholesale case composed of two 12-packs. That is not a 24-can consumer pack promised at every supermarket. The distributor also warns that package availability can vary by customer agreement and business segment.

For ordinary shopping, check the linked grocery listings with your own pickup location selected. Avoid treating the availability status from a search engine or another city as a local answer. Calling a nearby store with the full flavor name can be more useful than repeatedly refreshing an unconfigured delivery page.

Is Vanilla Howler discontinued now that summer is over?

Not on the evidence we reviewed. The launch was framed as a summer limited edition, and the distributor identifies it as an LTO. Neither source gives us a verified nationwide final sale date. October does not turn a seasonal marketing description into a precise discontinuation notice.

There are several separate questions: whether a promotion has ended, whether new batches are being produced, whether a distributor can reorder, and whether a store still has cans. A retailer page answers none of those completely on its own. Our report does not claim that the flavor is permanent or that October inventory is newly produced.

A dated brand statement or authenticated distribution notice could change the answer. Until then, limited edition with uncertain local stock is more accurate than either gone everywhere or here to stay. The same distinction is useful in our Coca-Cola Cherry Float availability guide, although that is a different product and must be assessed on its own sources.

How to read this status

The reliable conclusion is limited edition, with ongoing catalog visibility. We have not verified a national production stop, an exact last-sale date, or a statement making the flavor permanent.

An LTO can become hard to locate without a formal discontinuation announcement. Conversely, a product page may survive after stock runs down. Our status does not turn either kind of incomplete evidence into a promise about what a store can supply.

How to avoid buying the wrong MUG

Search for Vanilla Howler or MUG Floats, then confirm the sugar variant, package count and serving size. A generic MUG Root Beer result can be the standard drink rather than this special flavor. When ordering grocery delivery, check the substitution setting so a regular root beer is not silently treated as equivalent.

Compare cost per can, including delivery, before paying a specialty-market premium. A store listing for twelve cans is not directly comparable to a single-can offer or a wholesale case. We have not set a fair-market collector value for this release or verified any seller's current stock.

Frequently asked

Vanilla Howler questions

Is there a MUG Vanilla Howler Zero Sugar?

Yes. The official launch included a separate Zero Sugar version, and U.S. retail listings identify it by name.

Is Vanilla Howler the same as regular MUG Root Beer?

No. Vanilla Howler is the named float-inspired limited edition. Check for Floats and Vanilla Howler on the package.

Does a sold-out listing mean Vanilla Howler is discontinued?

No. A local stock message is not a national production notice. A confirmed U.S. end date was not established in the sources reviewed on October 5, 2026.
