Is Monster Ultra Red discontinued?

No. Monster currently presents Ultra Red as an active U.S. product, complete with a 16-ounce listing and ways to shop or find it nearby. Its spotty history on shelves is why the rumor still feels believable.

By Discontinued Club ResearchChecked September 11, 20268 minute read
The short answer

Monster Ultra Red is not currently discontinued in the United States.

As of September 11, 2026, Monster Energy maintains an active U.S. product page for Ultra Red. The page identifies a 16-ounce can, describes a mixed-berry flavor, and offers both a shopping link and a "Find Near Me" path. That is direct manufacturer evidence that Ultra Red belongs in the current category, even if it has been absent from stores near you.

What is the strongest evidence that Ultra Red is current?

The strongest source is Monster itself. Its official U.S. Ultra Red product page is active and written as a current product listing. Monster describes the drink as a crisp mixed-berry Ultra flavor and gives nutrition highlights for the 16-ounce can: zero sugar, 10 calories, and 140 milligrams of caffeine.

More important for the status question, the page includes actions to shop online and find the product nearby. A manufacturer-backed product page with current commerce and locator paths is stronger evidence than an old marketplace listing or archived image. It does not promise that every distributor carries Ultra Red, but it does tell us Monster is not presenting the product as retired.

For that reason, our status is Current. We would not use "Confirmed Discontinued" unless Monster announced the end of the product or removed it from its active range with other evidence pointing in the same direction.

Was Ultra Red discontinued before and then brought back?

Many shoppers remember a period when Ultra Red became extremely difficult to locate, and older online discussions often describe it as discontinued. The current official page establishes today's status, but it does not by itself provide a complete production timeline. We cannot responsibly use that page to claim Ultra Red was continuously distributed in every year or every market.

The useful answer is narrower: whatever happened during earlier gaps, Monster is currently marketing Ultra Red again in the United States. It may be a return, a broader relisting, or a product that never fully disappeared from every distribution channel. Those possibilities matter to historians, but they do not change the buying question today.

This is also why dates belong on status reports. A correct answer from several years ago can become wrong after a relaunch. Search snippets, old forum posts, and retailer pages may continue circulating long after the brand changes course.

Why can Ultra Red still be hard to find?

National product status and local shelf availability are different systems. Monster may list a drink nationally while regional distributors, individual chains, and local stores make narrower assortment decisions. A store with room for six Monster varieties will usually prioritize its fastest sellers. Less common Ultra flavors can disappear from that cooler without disappearing from Monster's catalog.

Online inventory can create its own confusion. A retailer might keep a product page after local stock reaches zero, while another retailer might hide a current item until a nearby warehouse receives it. Search results may show multipacks, imports, single cans, or old package designs together. None of those signals alone establishes national discontinuation.

Package transitions can add another layer. A shopper who remembers one can design may overlook the same flavor in updated artwork, while an older listing can remain indexed after its specific package has left production. Always compare the flavor name and description, not only the thumbnail.

How to search for Monster Ultra Red now

Begin with Monster's own product page and use its store-finder path with your ZIP code. Then search retailer sites for both "Monster Ultra Red" and "Ultra Red 16 oz." Check whether a result is available at a named store, available only for shipping, or simply part of a retailer's general catalog.

If the closest result is far away, expand the radius and check convenience stores, grocery chains, nutrition retailers, and beverage specialists separately. Distributor territories do not always line up neatly with state boundaries. A flavor can be missing from one chain while appearing at another across town.

When buying from a marketplace or collector store, confirm the quantity, can size, condition, expiration or production markings, and whether the cans are full. Ask whether the photograph shows the actual items. Older unopened energy drinks should be treated as collectibles when age and storage conditions are uncertain; a sealed can is not proof that its contents are suitable to consume.

What does Monster Ultra Red taste like?

Monster currently characterizes Ultra Red as a mixed-berry flavor with a crisp, refreshing profile. That description is intentionally broad rather than tied to one named fruit. The drink belongs to the zero-sugar Ultra line, so shoppers looking for it are usually trying to recover a particular berry profile rather than simply finding any red can.

That distinction matters because product colors are reused across categories and markets. A red package does not guarantee the Ultra Red formula, and a retailer's shortened title may omit the word "Ultra." Look for the complete product name and the zero-sugar Ultra branding.

Monster's current U.S. page lists 140 milligrams of caffeine, 10 calories, and zero grams of sugar per 16-ounce can. Formulas and labels can vary by country or change over time, so the can in hand remains the right source for dietary and ingredient decisions.
