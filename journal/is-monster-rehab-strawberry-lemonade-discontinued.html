Is Monster Rehab Strawberry Lemonade discontinued?

The red Rehab can and the newer patriotic Juice can share a flavor name, but they are not the same product. Here is the U.S. status check for the one that disappeared.

By Discontinued Club ResearchChecked September 21, 20267 minute read
The short answer

Reported discontinued in the U.S. The newer Juice drink is a separate product.

Monster Rehab Strawberry Lemonade was named in reporting about U.S. discontinuations planned for 2025. It is also absent from the Rehab section of Monster's current U.S. catalog, checked September 21, 2026. That supports a reported U.S. discontinuation, although we have not verified a manufacturer notice giving the final production date. Juice Monster Strawberry Lemonade, introduced in 2026, is a different line, not evidence that the red Rehab can returned.

What happened to Rehab Strawberry Lemonade?

In a November 14, 2024 report, Sporked writer Griffin Parker named Rehab Strawberry Lemonade among Monster products slated for discontinuation in 2025. He cited a discontinuation chart and said it did not include specific timetables. That distinction matters: the report identifies a planned exit, not the day every store sold its last can.

Our present-day cross-check is Monster's U.S. Rehab directory. At this review it lists Tea + Lemonade, Peach Tea, Wild Berry Tea, and Green Tea. Strawberry Lemonade is missing from that group. The wider American catalog instead places a Strawberry Lemonade product under Juice Monster.

Taken together, those sources support the reported U.S. departure. An omission by itself would be weaker evidence, and we have not independently verified the original chart with Monster. We also do not have evidence establishing why the company made the decision. Claims about poor sales, ingredient problems, or a safety issue should not be inferred from a discontinued label.

Is the new Juice Strawberry Lemonade the same drink?

No: the product line is different. Monster's May 4, 2026 announcement introduced Juice Monster Strawberry Lemonade as a new summer release in patriotic packaging. The announcement does not identify it as a relaunch of Rehab Strawberry Lemonade.

The current U.S. Juice product page describes a strawberry-and-lemonade drink with real juice flavor and lists a 16-ounce size. That page belongs to Juice Monster. The older red can pictured here says Rehab, which is the key identifier to look for when comparing search results.

If you want the exact drink you remember, do not shop by the words strawberry lemonade alone. Read the line name on the front and compare the nutrition and ingredient panels on the actual package. We have not conducted a side-by-side tasting or verified identical recipes, so we would not promise that the newer drink reproduces the older one.

This also prevents a misleading shopping experience: a current Juice listing should not be presented as available stock of the retired Rehab product. The two can share search terms without being interchangeable inventory.

Why are people noticing the change in 2026?

A reported assortment change and a shopper's last purchase rarely happen at the same time. A store can sell through a case well after its supplier stops replenishing it. Another shop may remove the shelf label sooner. Neither experience gives a reliable nationwide final-sale date.

This article is dated September 21, 2026 because that is when we checked the available evidence. The discontinuation reporting concerned 2025. We are not moving the event into 2026 simply because people are searching for answers now.

The practical question is whether a seller has the old Rehab drink or a different product with a familiar name. A recently created listing is not proof of recent manufacture. Likewise, a delivery arriving this week can contain older stock. Ask about the actual cans rather than treating the listing date as a production record.

Why is there still an official product page?

Monster's Puerto Rico product page still displays Rehab Strawberry Lemonade and describes it as non-carbonated. It is also the source of the single-can reference image used here. Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory, not a foreign country; Monster nevertheless maintains a separate regional website for it.

That surviving page is worth reporting, but it does not tell us when the pictured cans were made or which stores can reorder them today. We have not verified current production or local stock in Puerto Rico. It would be misleading to turn a regional webpage into a claim that the drink is still being manufactured there.

When a search returns an official-looking page, check the selected region and the product line before drawing a conclusion. A regional catalog can explain why the name remains searchable even when the main U.S. assortment no longer includes it. We are not claiming a worldwide discontinuation.

Can you still buy the old red Rehab can?

You can look for remaining cans on resale marketplaces such as eBay and at specialist drink retailers. We have not verified a live offer for this article, so these are places to search, not a promise that a seller currently has stock. Discontinued Club does not currently list Rehab Strawberry Lemonade for sale.

Use the complete search term Monster Rehab Strawberry Lemonade, then open the selected listing. Some marketplace pages combine several flavors under one headline. Make sure the available selection is the red Rehab can, not Juice Strawberry Lemonade or a different Rehab flavor.

Confirm the pack count, whether the cans are full or empty, and whether the photos show the actual items. Request a readable base or date-code photo if age matters to the purchase. Compare the complete delivered price; shipping can change the value of a single-can offer substantially.

A seller restocking a few cans does not establish a brand relaunch. Conversely, a discontinued flavor can still be a legitimate collectible. The important distinction is between a specific seller's remaining inventory and a manufacturer returning the product to normal distribution.

Will Rehab Strawberry Lemonade return?

We found no verified announcement of a U.S. Rehab Strawberry Lemonade return in the sources reviewed. There is no supported comeback date to give readers. The May 2026 Juice launch is an announcement about a different product line.

A stronger update would name Rehab explicitly and connect that announcement to renewed U.S. distribution. Until then, a red can appearing online, a reused product photo, or a familiar flavor name on a new package should not be described as a confirmed return.

For the broader picture, see our Monster discontinuation index and Aussie Lemonade status report. Our Rehab Green Tea rumor report covers a separate claim; it should not be used as evidence for Strawberry Lemonade's timeline.

How to read this status

We use reported U.S. discontinuation because a dated independent report and the current U.S. assortment point in the same direction. We are not presenting an authenticated factory notice or a verified last shipment date.

The market check is Monster's United States website. Regional pages, resale offers, and an older package in a shop are recorded as context, not treated as proof of national availability or a worldwide stop in production.

What buyers and collectors should check

Verify the exact flavor name, package design, can size, country labeling, condition, quantity, and the seller's photo before buying. A marketplace listing can combine an old image with newer inventory, and a foreign-market can may use similar colors for a different formula. The can shown here is one specific reference design, not a promise that every listing uses the same package.

For older full cans, treat the purchase as a collectible first. Storage history is rarely complete, and sealed cans can leak, swell, or change internally over time. A printed date and intact seal help identify an item, but they do not guarantee that an aged beverage remains suitable to drink.
