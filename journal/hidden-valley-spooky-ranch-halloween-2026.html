Spooky Ranch turns black for Halloween. The label does the glowing.

A familiar ranch flavor in an unfamiliar color, with a glow-in-the-dark label. Here is what changes, where it is sold and how long the seasonal window runs.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished October 25, 20264 minute read
The short answer

It is a Walmart-exclusive seasonal ranch, not a new chocolate flavor.

Hidden Valley describes Spooky Ranch as its familiar ranch taste with a black appearance created using fruit juice and cocoa. The 12 oz bottle has glow-in-the-dark packaging. Clorox gives a September 18 through November 1, 2026 Walmart window, while the launch release says supplies are limited. This October 23 edition was published and checked October 25.

The color changes. The flavor stays in ranch territory.

The official product page calls this a 12 oz condiment and dressing with a buttermilk, herb and spice profile. Fruit juice and cocoa provide the dark color. The presence of cocoa is not a claim that this is a sweet chocolate sauce or a dessert-flavored ranch.

Hidden Valley is selling a visual Halloween variation on a familiar dressing. That distinction matters if a dramatic bottle photo is the first thing you see: the black color is the novelty, rather than a newly announced spicy or chocolate flavor. We have not tasted it, so this is an explanation of the manufacturer description, not a review.

For a snack spread, the practical question is whether you want that visual effect. The brand suggests the usual ranch uses, including vegetables, wings and pizza. There is no need to invent a different flavor profile to explain why the Halloween bottle looks different from the everyday one.

What actually glows in the dark?

Clorox's September 27 behind-the-scenes article identifies a special varnish on the label. It makes the Spooky lettering and jack-o'-lantern artwork glow in the dark. The effect is on the packaging, not an edible glow effect in the dressing.

That is a useful distinction for both shoppers and package collectors. A photo of a luminous bottle is not evidence that a bowl of ranch will illuminate a table. Save the packaging reference if that is the part you are interested in, and treat the condiment as a separate food product with its own label directions.

Walmart exclusive, with a window through November 1

The company's dated article gives a September 18 through November 1, 2026 availability window exclusively at Walmart. The September 24 launch announcement describes a limited run while supplies last and gives a suggested retail price of $3.97. That is an announced suggested price, not a verified quote from your local store today.

Those two pieces of information should be read together: there is a planned seasonal window, but inventory can run out before it closes. We have not verified local Walmart stock. Check pickup or delivery availability for your own location and confirm the seller and bottle size before making a trip.

There is also a wording mismatch on the official product page: its main description still says coming soon, while its FAQ describes Walmart availability. The dated September announcements provide the clearer launch timeline. We would not read that leftover phrase as evidence that an October launch is still pending.

Does November 1 mean it is permanently discontinued?

No. A planned retail window does not tell us the exact final manufacturing date, guarantee that every remaining bottle disappears that night, or rule out a future Halloween return. We have not found a confirmed return announcement in the sources used for this guide.

The right description is limited Halloween edition. A bottle listed after the window could be remaining inventory. That would not, by itself, establish a new production run. Likewise, a sold-out local page would tell you about that offer, not prove that the brand has permanently retired the idea.

How to read this status

Limited Halloween edition describes the announced seasonal release, not a permanent lineup addition. November 1 is the end of the company-stated retail window; while-supplies-last terms still apply. We do not treat either as a national inventory countdown.

Before adding it to a Halloween shopping list

Confirm the 12 oz Spooky Ranch bottle rather than an ordinary ranch listing with seasonal search keywords. Compare the total delivered price with the announced suggested price, and check the actual back label for ingredients, dates and storage directions. This journal post does not offer the product for sale.

Frequently asked

Hidden Valley Spooky Ranch questions

Is Spooky Ranch chocolate flavored?

Hidden Valley describes the familiar ranch profile. Cocoa and fruit juice are used for the black color; the brand does not describe it as a chocolate-flavored dressing.

Does the ranch itself glow?

The documented glow effect is a varnish on parts of the bottle label, including lettering and Halloween artwork.

Where is Spooky Ranch sold?

It is a Walmart-exclusive 12 oz Halloween edition. Local inventory and current prices were not verified for this guide.

When does the seasonal window end?

Clorox states November 1, 2026. Limited supplies may sell out sooner, and that date does not establish permanent discontinuation.
