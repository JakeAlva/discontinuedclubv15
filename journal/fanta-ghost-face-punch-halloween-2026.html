Fanta Ghost Face Punch is real. What is in the can?

The mask is the first thing you notice. The more useful question is whether you are looking at a new soda or an old flavor in a Halloween costume.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished October 1, 20263 minute read
The short answer

Ghost Face Punch is a new flavor, not just a new label.

Coca-Cola announced the limited-edition collaboration on August 26, 2026. The current U.S. Halloween collection includes Ghost Face Punch, with a dark-berry flavor description. It is an official product, not a fan-made concept.

What flavor is Ghost Face Punch?

The U.S. product page describes tangy dark berries. It does not name a precise blend of individual fruits. We would not turn the purple color into a confirmed grape recipe, or invent a list of berries the label does not provide.

That leaves room for a real taste review later. A brand description tells us the intended profile; it cannot tell us how sweet, sharp or familiar the drink will seem to every reader. We have not opened a can for this report.

Is it zero sugar or caffeine-free?

For the 12-fluid-ounce U.S. can, the official entry lists 160 calories, 42 grams of total sugars and no caffeine. This is not a zero-sugar soda. Those figures describe that formula and serving, not every package sold under the Fanta brand.

When comparing an import with a domestic can, use the label on each item. A matching flavor name does not establish an identical recipe, serving size or nutrition panel across countries. Likewise, a seller using one stock image for several sizes should specify which container the order includes.

Not every Halloween Fanta can is a new flavor

The corporate announcement places Ghost Face in Fanta's Haunted Universe through a collaboration with Fun World and Paramount+. Collectible character packaging is part of the wider campaign.

The U.S. collection also shows Orange, Orange Zero, Pineapple and Strawberry. That distinction matters to both drinkers and collectors: someone seeking a new recipe and someone seeking a seasonal design may be shopping for different things.

Our rule is straightforward. Identify the flavor from the printed product name, and identify the collectible from the actual package. Treating those as separate questions avoids describing every redesigned can as a new drink.

Where is it available, and when does it end?

Fanta's U.S. Halloween page directs shoppers to major retailers and online for specially marked packs. It does not provide a guarantee for every ZIP code. Use the selected store or delivery address when checking an offer.

The same page gives November 1, 2026 as the end of the U.S. instant-win promotion. That is a game deadline. It is not a stated production cutoff, an expiry date for a can or a promise that the flavor will remain on shelves until then.

We have not verified an exact final retail date or a permanent-lineup announcement. Before paying a scarcity premium, check ordinary retail availability. The word limited describes a release strategy; it does not, by itself, prove the remaining supply is rare.

How to read this status

We are covering this as a launch, not calling it discontinued. A limited edition can be on sale now without being promised a permanent place in the range.

A future article about its disappearance would need later evidence. The fact that a product was designed around Halloween is not enough to declare that normal distribution has already stopped.

For collectors, the exact can matters

Ask a seller whether the photo shows the actual package, which market it was made for, and whether the listing is for a full can or an empty display collectible. Compare the total delivered price and quantity, not just the large number in the search result.
