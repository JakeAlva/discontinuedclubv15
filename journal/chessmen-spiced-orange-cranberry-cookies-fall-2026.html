Chessmen Spiced Orange Cranberry: the new fall cookie to look for

Pepperidge Farm is taking Chessmen beyond the usual butter-cookie aisle with a seasonal orange-and-cranberry edition. Here is how to find the right bag.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished October 6, 20265 minute read
The short answer

A confirmed seasonal release, not a discontinued cookie comeback.

Chessmen Spiced Orange Cranberry is a new limited-edition fall 2026 flavor, confirmed in Campbell's September 28 seasonal lineup. U.S. retailer records identify a 6.4-ounce bag. Some stores file it under Mulling Spice, so the full product description matters when searching.

What is the new Chessmen flavor?

Campbell's September 28 fall lineup names Chessmen Spiced Orange Cranberry as a new addition to Pepperidge Farm's Autumn Exclusives collection. The concept combines the recognizable butter-cookie format with citrus, cranberry and spice notes. It is a seasonal extension, not a replacement announcement for the original cookie.

For readers who associate fall snacks exclusively with pumpkin, that makes this one worth noticing: the package name points to a different flavor combination. We have not tasted the cookies, so claims about how strong the orange, tartness or spice actually tastes would be premature.

Did it just launch in October?

No. Our article is an October update, not a claim that the cookie debuted today. Bake Magazine reported the launch on August 14, 2026, identifying Walmart, Target and Kroger as retail destinations and a suggested price of $5.89. Campbell's later fall roundup provides direct manufacturer confirmation.

Those dates help separate a new discovery from a brand-new release. A shopper seeing it for the first time this week may simply be encountering an assortment that arrived earlier elsewhere. We have not verified a single date on which every U.S. store stocked it.

The limited-edition label does not supply an exact disappearance date. Neither the manufacturer roundup nor the retail records reviewed here establishes when the final bag will leave shelves or whether the flavor will return next autumn.

What size is the bag?

Target's product record identifies a 6.4-ounce bag with 21 cookies. Its listing showed $4.59 when checked, illustrating why an earlier suggested retail price should not be treated as a universal checkout price. Local prices and promotions can differ.

The package count is useful when comparing an individual bag with an online bundle. Check whether the seller is quoting one bag or several, and whether shipping changes the apparent saving. We are not treating a search result price as a promise to every reader.

Target also publishes an ingredient and allergen panel. Use the label on the bag you actually buy for dietary decisions; a seasonal design or the familiar Chessmen name is not enough to establish that it fits your needs.

Why does it show up as Mulling Spice?

Walmart's record is headed Mulling Spice, while its item details identify Spiced Orange Cranberry. Shaw's similarly uses Mulling Spices in the title but describes the same named 6.4-ounce flavor in the body.

That is a catalog naming mismatch, not evidence that we have found a separate second flavor. The practical lesson is to open the product details instead of judging the result by its short search title. Match the name on the package and its size before ordering.

It also explains why searching the exact wording from a news headline may miss a retailer page. Trying the alternate catalog wording can help locate the record, but it still will not confirm that a chosen store has a bag available for pickup.

What else is in the fall lineup?

The same manufacturer roundup includes new Snack Factory Apple Spice White Creme Pretzel Crisps, alongside returning Pumpkin Spice White Creme Pretzel Crisps, Milano Pumpkin Spice, and Pumpkin Cheesecake soft-baked cookies. These are separate products, not additional varieties of the new Chessmen bag.

Whole Foods lists the Apple Spice Pretzel Crisps in a 4-ounce bag and describes apple-flavored white creme with a spice-flavored drizzle. That gives shoppers another specific seasonal name to look for without assuming every retailer carries the complete lineup.

For more product news beyond drinks, our journal also follows new candy and snack releases. We keep release reports separate from reviews: a verified launch is something we can document, while a recommendation about taste requires actually trying the product.

How to read this status

Limited edition describes the intended sales window, not proof that an item has already disappeared. At this check, the manufacturer identifies this as part of its fall assortment. We found no basis for calling the entire Chessmen brand discontinued.

A seasonal flavor can become difficult to locate without a formal nationwide end announcement. We will distinguish any later change in the flavor's status from the availability of ordinary Chessmen butter cookies.

Use the exact bag name when checking a store

Search Spiced Orange Cranberry first, then Mulling Spice if the result does not appear. Match the 6.4-ounce size and package picture before checking out. Do not assume a plain Chessmen result or a different seasonal cookie is an equivalent substitution.

Select your store before making a trip, and compare price per ounce when looking at multipacks. This article links retail records for identification, not as a promise that any particular branch has inventory today.
