Starry Cranberry Blizz is back on the holiday calendar.

The cranberry-and-lemon-lime soda has a current seasonal listing. That does not turn every older can online into this year's stock.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished October 26, 20263 minute read
The short answer

A 2026 return is supported; nationwide stock is not guaranteed.

Brown Bottling Group lists Starry Cranberry Blizz Zero Sugar for October 19 through December 11, 2026 in its Mississippi service territory. Treat that as a regional promotional window, not a promise that every U.S. store has it or that all stock disappears on December 12.

What the dated announcement actually covers

The bottler's winter roundup describes the returning flavor as a cranberry twist on lemon-lime soda and lists can twelve-packs and two-liter bottles. Its October-to-December schedule covers the service territory named in the announcement. Store selection and sell-through can still differ inside that region.

A useful next step is to check a specific retailer and package size, then confirm whether the result is actually available for pickup or delivery. A search result saying a flavor exists is not the same as a store holding a sellable package today. Avoid making a special trip based only on an undated product page.

A supplier case is not necessarily one retail pack

PepsiCo Partners lists its can case as 24 twelve-ounce cans, arranged as two twelve-packs. Its two-liter listing is an eight-bottle case. Both are marked limited-time products. Those are supplier ordering units, not a statement that a supermarket only sells complete cases.

Read the unit attached to the price. One can, one twelve-pack and one supplier case are three different quantities even when the product name is identical. Compare the delivered total and number of containers, not just the first large number on a search card.

Our 2025 can is a separate offer

Discontinued Club's Cranberry Blizz product page identifies a single full, unopened 12 oz can from 2025. A current flavor return does not change that listing's year, make the can newly produced, or verify its freshness. Collectors should use the listing and request printed-date details before buying.

An unopened seal is not a freshness guarantee. If your goal is a current-season drink rather than an older packaging collectible, start with current retail stock and inspect its label. If the packaging is the attraction, ask about the exact design and cosmetic condition. Those are different buying goals, and the same search can show both.

How to read this status

The supported conclusion is a seasonal return of the Zero Sugar product. This report does not establish a permanent year-round launch or confirm a regular-sugar version.

Check the version, not just the flavor name

Keep Zero Sugar, container size, pack count and listing year in view. We have not conducted a taste comparison with earlier releases and are not claiming that the recipe is unchanged.
