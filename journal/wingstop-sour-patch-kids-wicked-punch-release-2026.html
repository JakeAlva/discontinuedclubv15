Sour Patch Kids wings are real. Here is when Wingstop serves them.

The candy crossover is confirmed, but a social post is not a signal to head to the restaurant just yet.

By Discontinued Club ResearchPublished October 15, 20262 minute read
The short answer

Confirmed, but not yet in its announced ordering window.

Wingstop has announced the collaboration. As of our October 15 check, both its member-access date and wider launch are still ahead.

When do the Sour Patch Kids wings come out?

Wingstop's October 13 announcement gives Club Wingstop members access starting October 23. The wider U.S. launch is October 27, with the promotion scheduled through November 5 at participating locations, while supplies last.

For planning purposes, save the ordering date that applies to you rather than the day you first saw the news. Joining a program, seeing a campaign image and being able to order the item are three different things. Check the selected restaurant in the app before making a special trip.

What is Wicked Punch?

The brand describes a red-berry glaze with a sour-to-sweet profile, finished with Sour Patch Kids Bitz and available across its proteins. It directs customers to its app or website to order.

We have not tasted it, so we cannot tell you whether the candy balances the savory base or overwhelms it. The photograph establishes the look of the promotional serving, not a verdict on the flavor. For anyone intrigued but uncertain, our suggestion is to try a modest order before making it the centerpiece of a group meal.

What to check before ordering

Confirm the actual item, protein, quantity and total shown by your chosen restaurant. A campaign photograph can include sides or props that are not part of the order. Do not use the photo alone to decide what you are paying for.

For allergies or ingredient restrictions, check the complete current product information with the restaurant, including the topping and sauce. A familiar candy name does not establish the ingredients or preparation conditions of the finished meal.

This also makes a useful comparison with our Dunkin Halloween guide: when a promotion has several dates, the practical details matter more than the launch image.

A wing flavor is not a new bag of candy

The collaboration name can look like a candy launch when it appears without context in a search result. This report concerns a restaurant menu offering. It does not establish a new retail bag of candy, a supermarket sauce or a change to the regular Sour Patch Kids range.

Likewise, a promotional image is not evidence that someone has already tried an ordinary customer order. Before taking a reaction as a review, look for a clear explanation of what was sampled and when. We have no firsthand tasting to add here, and have not scored the product.

For now, the useful decision is whether the combination sounds interesting enough to check when ordering opens. Our recommendation is to keep curiosity separate from a verdict: an unusual pairing is neither automatically good nor automatically a gimmick. The taste question deserves an actual tasting, not an opinion dressed up as one.

How to read this status

An announced end date is not a guarantee of stock until that date. Nor does the end of one limited promotion establish that a flavor can never return.
