Is Coca-Cola Holiday Creamy Vanilla back for 2026?

An Oregon shopper reports finding the seasonal vanilla Coke again. Here is what that sighting establishes, and what it does not.

By Discontinued Club ResearchChecked October 6, 20265 minute read
The short answer

A U.S. sighting is reported. A nationwide date remains unverified.

As of October 6, 2026, a dated shopper report places regular and Zero Sugar Holiday Creamy Vanilla at an Oregon WinCo. We have not independently verified that store's stock or a nationwide U.S. 2026 rollout date. Separately, official announcements confirm fall launches in Great Britain and the Netherlands.

What is the latest U.S. sighting?

An October 5 post in Reddit's soda community describes finding the holiday lineup at a WinCo on Portland's east side. The poster identifies both regular and Zero Sugar Creamy Vanilla alongside Sprite Winter Spiced Cranberry. That is a specific, dated shopping lead rather than an undated repost.

It is still a consumer account. We did not visit the store, check its inventory system, or establish the production dates on those packages. Our conclusion is therefore limited: there is a recent U.S. report worth checking locally, not proof that every supermarket has started selling a new run.

The difference matters when a comeback headline travels faster than distribution. A reader in Illinois should not treat one Oregon sighting as a guarantee that a nearby store has the same assortment.

Which 2026 launches are officially confirmed?

Coca-Cola Europacific Partners announced the British launch on September 28. It describes a limited-time release in Original Taste and Zero Sugar, with cans, multipacks and bottles. That date belongs to Great Britain; it is not an American on-sale date.

Coca-Cola's Dutch announcement gives a different window: availability from week 36 of 2026 through March 2027, with regular and Zero Sugar options. Again, that schedule applies to the Netherlands. It cannot be used as a U.S. end date.

These announcements explain why apparently fresh Creamy Vanilla news can refer to different countries at once. Our Pitch Black UK launch report makes the same distinction: a real overseas launch does not automatically become a domestic return.

Is there a Zero Sugar version?

Yes, this is an established variant, not simply a fan mockup. Coca-Cola Beverages Florida's holiday flavor guide identifies Zero Sugar options for Creamy Vanilla and Winter Spiced Cranberry. We use that guide to establish the variants, not as a newly dated nationwide 2026 launch notice.

The recent Oregon poster also reports both versions, but one version being stocked nearby does not guarantee the other is. When asking a store to check, specify regular or Zero Sugar as well as the pack size. Product names that differ by just those two words can lead to the wrong order.

Where should U.S. shoppers look?

Target has a U.S. 12-pack product record, and Walmart has a corresponding 12-ounce, 12-pack listing. These are useful starting points for identifying the product, but neither record alone establishes fresh inventory at your location.

Choose the actual pickup store before relying on a stock message. For shipping, check who sells and fulfills the order, the quantity, and the total price. A marketplace offer, a cached search result and a store-operated grocery listing can describe very different buying options.

We are intentionally not publishing one national price: the retailer records can change with location, seller and promotions. A locally confirmed shelf tag is better evidence for your purchase than an old price repeated in a return story.

Is it the same as regular Vanilla Coke?

Holiday Creamy Vanilla is marketed as its own seasonal product. That establishes its identity, not an independently tested claim about how much creamier it tastes. We have not compared the recipes or conducted a side-by-side tasting, so this report does not rank it against regular Vanilla Coke.

For now, the practical answer to "is it back?" is to check local inventory using the exact name. The overseas launches are confirmed, the U.S. sighting is reported, and a precise nationwide U.S. calendar remains unverified at this update.

How to read this status

Reported is the important word. An individual sighting is a useful lead, but it cannot tell us how many states have received deliveries, whether every package is from a new run, or how long stores will carry it.

We are not labeling this flavor permanently discontinued, and we are not promising a national comeback on a date we cannot document. Seasonal availability and nationwide distribution are separate questions.
