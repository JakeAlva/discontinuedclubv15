Research lanes
A clearer way to follow discontinuation
Journal reports carry a visible status so readers can tell confirmed information from a watch signal or an unverified rumor.
Confirmed

Official brand statements and verified retailer changes

Announcements, product pages, press releases, and direct confirmation belong here. Sources will be linked and dated.

Watch

Availability signals worth paying attention to

Disappearing store listings, sustained shelf gaps, package transitions, and sharp regional changes can signal a product shift.

Rumor

Community speculation, clearly labeled

Interesting chatter can begin an investigation, but it will never be written as confirmed news without evidence.

Editorial standard

Useful when you are standing in a store wondering if this is the last time you will see it.

Each report starts with the question customers actually ask: "Is this flavor discontinued?" The answer includes the evidence behind it, the date it was checked, and a clear path to current inventory when the product is available.

Coverage focuses on soda, energy drinks, snacks, and treats, with additional categories guided by meaningful customer demand.

No manufactured certainty.

Discontinued Club will not call something discontinued just because it is hard to find. Scarcity can come from seasonal distribution, production timing, regional availability, or a packaging transition. The status should match the evidence.
